## The shape of the problem

Most teams start with a job runner because the first few tasks are obvious. Then the work accumulates timing constraints, retries, side effects, and multiple owners. At that point, you no longer have a lightweight background task. You have the beginning of a workflow engine, whether you planned one or not.

## The mistake I see often

We keep the mental model of a queue while the operational model has already grown into something else. That mismatch shows up as vague retry behavior, missing ownership boundaries, and jobs that technically run but are no longer explainable.

## A better framing

Treat long-running or high-impact background work like a product surface. Define states, clarify transitions, record enough metadata for operators, and decide what idempotency means before incidents force the answer.
